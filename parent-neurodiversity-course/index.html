03 / Everyone around the team

This is for
more than one family.

Families supporting a neurodivergent child need practical help. Everyone else can learn to make football easier to be part of.

The proposed course welcomes parents, carers, guardians and spectators whether or not their own child is neurodivergent. Nobody needs a diagnosis to access it, and no family should have to disclose private information to explain a child’s behaviour to the touchline.

01

Families of neurodivergent children

Prepare for changes, spot rising pressure, support recovery, and share what helps with a coach in a short, usable way.

02

Other parents and supporters

Understand that behaviour can mean different things. Replace snap judgements with respectful action, without asking a family for an explanation.

03

The child at the centre

Have their preferences heard where appropriate. Support follows the individual child, rather than a checklist attached to a label.

04 / What you will explore

Six short
chapters.

Explore each of the six planned lessons. Open a chapter for its focus and a practical example. Lesson 05 will be tested first.

Recognise how change, noise, waiting or uncertainty may feel different from one child to another. Sam is one example, with contrasting stories so no single child becomes a template.
Plan for venue changes, kit and sensory discomfort, transitions, and clear information before training or match day.
Spot early signs without assuming what they mean. Reduce immediate pressure, support recovery, and work out what can wait until later.
Recognise how touchline comments, shouting and reactions to a referee’s decision affect children. Practise respectful responses to unkind assumptions about another child, and take a moment to steady yourself after a difficult match. The referee is part of the game too: adults can disagree with a call without abusing an official or adding pressure to a child.
First lesson to be tested
Practise a short message, decide what to share, and agree one realistic next step. A coach can explain what is possible and when they will check back. A short branch covers what another parent or spectator can do if they witness comments or exclusion.
Check what helped, prepare for a new coach or age group, and consider other routes when a club is not the right fit.

05 / How you would learn

At the right
time. In the right
format.

01

Self-paced online core

Short lessons, 60-second summaries, a running grassroots scenario and resources adults can use on a phone. They can go straight to the help they need; finishing every chapter is not the measure of success.

02

Live online conversation

Occasional moderated sessions would let families explore shared questions with a facilitator. They would complement the core learning and follow clear safeguarding boundaries.

The course can be revisited before a match, after a difficult moment or when a new coach takes over. Live online discussion adds a human route for questions.

06 / Tools to take away

Practical help
to take away.

↗ 01

What helps my child

A simple, child-informed note about what the player enjoys, what becomes hard and what helps. The family keeps control of the note and decides whether to share it.

↗ 02

A coach message

A short phrase bank helps a parent open a conversation without having to tell the whole story on the touchline.

↗ 03

A coach response sheet

One page for a volunteer coach: acknowledge the note, try one practical step, protect privacy and agree when to review.

↗ 04

A touchline guide

Simple actions for every supporter when a child seems overwhelmed, comments become unkind or a referee makes a decision they dislike.

Privacy by design. The planned course does not ask families to submit a child’s health details to use its tools. Sharing information with a coach is the family’s choice.

10 / Common questions

Good to know.

Does my child need a diagnosis?

No. The course focuses on what a child experiences and what helps. Families awaiting assessment or without a formal label are welcome.

Is it only for families of neurodivergent children?

No. Any parent, carer, guardian or spectator can learn to respond better to another child’s experience without asking that child’s family to explain it.

Is this an in-person course?

The planned core is self-paced online learning, with occasional live online discussion. There is no face-to-face parent course in this plan.

Will families have to share details about their child?

No. The family chooses whether to share a note with a coach. The planned course tools do not require uploading a child’s health details.

Can I enrol now?

Not yet. The first lesson will be tested before the full course is released. Clubs and County FAs can contact FMHA about development and future access.
