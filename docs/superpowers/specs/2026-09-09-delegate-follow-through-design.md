# Delegate Follow-Through Design

## Purpose

Turn the Managing Anger delegate hub from a reference library into a practical follow-through system. Delegates should be able to decide what to do next, record commitments on their own device, respond safely to low-level escalation, handle discriminatory language with children, and follow a clear post-incident process.

## Scope

The work applies only to the files served from `assets.thefmha.com` in this repository. The commercial training page and PowerPoint files are out of scope.

## Privacy model

All entered information stays in the delegate's browser. The pages may use `localStorage` to retain drafts on that device and `window.print()` for Print or Save PDF. They must not use forms with network actions, `fetch`, analytics events, email submission, or any other transmission of entered information. Each tool must explain this plainly and provide a clear-device-data control.

## Experience

The hub gains a compact Start Here pathway with four time-based steps. It continues to expose the existing reference tools, but adds a Post-Incident and Aftercare Kit and updates the implementation-guide description to reflect its expanded purpose.

The Club Behaviour Implementation Guide becomes a combined Club Action and Repair Plan. It includes the Four Pillars audit, red/amber/green status, evidence, first action, owner, target date, the three session commitments, restorative questions, reporting boundaries, review date, draft persistence, clearing and printing.

Sideline Signals gains a Safe Pattern Interruption section. It limits neutral interruption techniques to low-level escalation, provides usable examples, and makes the switch to safety and reporting explicit at threatening or physical levels.

Junior Player Cards gains a sixth Words Carry Weight card. Children receive a short Pause, Ask, Tell route. Adults receive a calm response sequence that stops the language, supports the person targeted, checks understanding privately, explains impact, records exact words and follows club or safeguarding procedures.

The Post-Incident and Aftercare Kit contains an incident record, a reporting and safeguarding boundary, support for the affected person, a young-referee check-in, welfare-officer aftercare, restorative follow-up and a review record. It follows the same device-only persistence and print model.

The browser reference deck is corrected where claims are inconsistent, absolute or unsupported. It will avoid fixed multipliers for emotional processing, remove the unsourced 5,000-reactions and 40-per-cent claims, soften physiological claims, remove the anger-equals-choice distinction, remove the "never harmful" absolute, and avoid asserting that post-incident reviews guarantee lower recurrence.

## Safety boundaries

Restorative conversations happen after immediate safety and formal reporting decisions. They do not replace FA discipline, safeguarding procedures, police contact or emergency action. No resource should encourage a delegate to intervene directly in threatening or physical behaviour.

Children must not be publicly interrogated or shamed about discriminatory language. Claimed lack of understanding may change the educational response, but it does not remove the need to support the person targeted or follow reporting procedures.

## Validation

Automated static checks will verify links, required content, device-only behaviour and removal of identified risky claims. Browser checks will cover desktop and mobile layouts, interactive controls, persistence, clear-data behaviour and print controls. Every hub link must resolve on the deployed domain.
