Football can ask a lot before a ball is even kicked.

A late venue change. Several instructions delivered at once. A noisy touchline, an unfamiliar coach or a decision that feels impossible to process in the moment. These are ordinary parts of grassroots football, but they do not land in the same way for every player.

Behaviour is often the first thing adults notice and the last thing they examine. This session slows that moment down. It helps you consider communication, sensory load, uncertainty, emotional regulation and the football environment without treating any single response as proof of a condition.

The aim is not to turn coaches or volunteers into clinicians. It is to help the adults around the game respond with more curiosity, clarity and consistency.

Founder's note

Support cannot wait for a diagnosis.

Recognition and referrals for ADHD and autism have risen sharply, while assessment pathways have struggled to keep pace. Many children and families are waiting months or years for answers and support.

Football clubs are already supporting neurodivergent players and their families, often without consistent training or practical guidance. This session helps the adults around the game respond to the person in front of them, without asking coaches or volunteers to diagnose anyone.

Danny Matharu, Founder of The Football Mental Health Alliance

Six parts, one evening

A full course, not a quick awareness talk.

Each part connects understanding to the decisions adults make around training, match day and club life.

  1. 01

    Introduction to neurodiversity

    Build a grounded understanding of neurodiversity and why different ways of processing information can shape a person's experience of football.

    What we work through
    • What neurodiversity means, and what it does not mean.
    • Why support should respond to the person rather than wait for a label.
    • How strengths, needs and context can exist together.

  2. 02

    Understanding neurodivergent behaviour in football

    Explore what may sit beneath behaviour that is too quickly described as rude, lazy, distracted or disruptive.

    What we work through
    • Communication differences, attention and executive-function demands.
    • Sensory load, uncertainty, transitions and emotional regulation.
    • How to stay curious without making assumptions or attempting diagnosis.

  3. 03

    Strategies for coaches

    Turn understanding into coaching habits that help more players know what is happening, what is expected and what comes next.

    What we work through
    • Clearer instructions, demonstrations and checks for understanding.
    • Predictable session structures with flexible routes through an activity.
    • Practical adjustments to space, waiting, feedback and transitions.

  4. 04

    The role of referees and officials

    Consider how the pace, language and pressure of match day can affect participation, communication and decision-making.

    What we work through
    • Communicating decisions without unnecessarily escalating the moment.
    • Recognising overload while maintaining fair and consistent boundaries.
    • Helping coaches, officials and welfare leads work from a shared plan.

  5. 05

    Supporting families and players

    Create more useful conversations with the people who understand the player best, while respecting privacy and individual preference.

    What we work through
    • Asking what helps rather than asking for proof of diagnosis.
    • Agreeing useful information, boundaries and points of contact.
    • Planning for changes, setbacks and difficult football moments together.

  6. LIVE

    Interactive discussion, practical interventions and Q&A

    Work through recognisable grassroots situations, test practical responses and bring questions from your own role or club.

    What we work through
    • Scenario-led discussion rather than abstract theory alone.
    • Space to examine what a proportionate response could look like.
    • Signposting for needs that sit beyond the scope of this training.

Who it is for

Anyone helping to shape the club environment.

No previous neurodiversity training is required. You do not need a diagnosis, and neither does a player before adults can listen, learn and consider reasonable support.

This training supports informed, inclusive football practice. It does not diagnose individuals or replace safeguarding, clinical, legal or emergency support.
