Post-Incident and Aftercare Kit

Capture facts while they are fresh, make support visible and close the loop. This is a practical working note, not an official reporting system.

Deal with immediate risk first. Call 999 where somebody is in immediate danger or needs urgent medical help. Safeguarding concerns, children, threats, discriminatory abuse and physical contact must follow the appropriate club, County FA, FA, police or emergency procedure. This page does not replace any of them.

Step 1

Immediate safety check

Do this before taking statements or asking people to repair a relationship.

Step 2

Incident record

Record what you saw or heard. Mark second-hand information clearly. Avoid diagnosis, motive and loaded labels.

Step 3

Support the people, not just the process

A tidy report is not aftercare. Name the contact, the next check-in and what the person actually needs.

Affected person

Offer choice and privacy. Do not require a meeting or apology as the price of support.

  • Ask what would help now.
  • Explain what will happen next.
  • Agree a safe contact and check-in.

Young referee

Check welfare away from the crowd. Involve their referee support contact, parent or guardian and safeguarding lead as appropriate.

  • Do not ask them to minimise abuse.
  • Confirm who will handle reporting.
  • Check in after the immediate adrenaline has passed.

Welfare officer aftercare

The person handling the incident may also need support, supervision and a proper handover.

  • Name a second club contact.
  • Protect rest and confidentiality.
  • Review workload after serious incidents.

Step 4

Reporting route and ownership

Record the official route used. Do not use this local page as the only copy of a report that your procedure requires elsewhere.

Step 5

Restorative follow-up

Only consider this when it is safe, voluntary and does not interfere with safeguarding, disciplinary or criminal procedures.

Step 6

Review and close

Check whether support happened, reporting was completed and the club learned something concrete.
